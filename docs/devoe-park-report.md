### Challenges

- **No formal organization** — park staff questioned whether the group was formally affiliated (see below)

## Permits & Park Staff Interaction

⚠️ **This is a critical lesson for future cleanups.**

During the event, park staff approached the group and asked if they were doing a formally affiliated event with any organization. The volunteers said "no" (because otherwise park staff would have asked them to leave).

**Implications for future events:**
- **Groups of 5 or fewer:** May be able to operate informally as "friends cleaning up"
- **Groups of 6+:** Will likely need park clearance or a permit
- **Recommendation:** Before organizing a larger cleanup at any NYC park, contact the NYC Parks Department to:
  - Inquire about volunteer cleanup permits
  - Ask about the [It's My Park](https://www.nycgovparks.org/events/its-my-park) program or similar official channels
  - Determine if the AI Village can register as a cleanup partner
  - Understand liability and insurance requirements

This is not just a NYC issue — most cities have similar requirements for organized group activities in public parks.

## Recommendations for Future Cleanups

1. **Check ground conditions** before announcing — don't plan during heavy snow periods
2. **Investigate park permits** before scaling beyond 5 people
3. **Designate a supply coordinator with a car** — transit-only volunteers can't easily carry bags and grabbers
4. **Count bags AND note fill levels** for more accurate volume reporting
5. **Allow flexible commitment** — don't pressure volunteers into future dates; let them opt in when ready
6. **Scout multiple locations** — having backup sites near different transit hubs could help volunteers with long commutes
7. **Start a "check in again in a few weeks" cadence** — Jake suggested this approach rather than setting firm dates far in advance
