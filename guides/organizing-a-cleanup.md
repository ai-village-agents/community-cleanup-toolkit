# How to Organize a Community Cleanup Event

## Overview
Start planning **4–6 weeks out** for the smoothest experience. This guide walks through each phase with practical steps and real lessons from Devoe Park.

## Phase 1: Research & Planning (4–6 weeks before)
- **Choose a location:** Look for visible litter, public access, and community significance.
- **Scout the site:** Document current conditions with photos and notes.
- **Check if permits are needed:** Requirements vary by city. Some parks departments welcome cleanups, others require permits or coordination.
- **Identify partners:** Local community groups, parks conservancies, neighborhood associations, and schools can help recruit volunteers and share supplies.
- **Set a date:** Weekends work best. Avoid major holidays. Check weather forecasts closer to the date.

## Phase 2: Logistics (2–4 weeks before)
- **Create a volunteer signup form:** Reference our signup template.
- **Gather supplies:**
  - Trash bags (30-gallon recommended)
  - Grabbers/litter pickers
  - Gloves
  - Hand sanitizer
  - First aid kit
  - Sunscreen
- **Plan meeting point and parking/transit info.**
- **Designate roles:** organizer, safety lead, photo documentarian, supply manager.
- **Arrange trash disposal:** Contact the parks department or sanitation services for pickup instructions.

## Phase 3: Outreach (2–3 weeks before)
- **Use outreach templates:** Reference our outreach template.
- **Reach out to local community groups.**
- **Post on social media, community boards, and neighborhood apps.**
- **Send confirmation emails:** One week before and the day before the cleanup.

## Phase 4: Event Day
Follow the day-of checklist guide for setup, safety briefing, documentation, and wrap-up.

## Phase 5: Follow-Up (within 1 week)
- **Send thank-you messages.**
- **Complete the post-event report.**
- **Share results on social media.**
- **Plan the next event.**

## Lessons from Devoe Park
- Expect **~50% of signups** to actually attend.
- A wheelchair can double as a mobile supply station.
- Even small groups (5 people) can make dramatic visible impact in 1 hour.
- Local residents may approach you during the cleanup — be welcoming, they may join future events.

## Budget Considerations
Many cleanups can be done for free or very cheap. Grabbers ($10–15 each) are the biggest investment. Trash bags are inexpensive, and some organizations will donate supplies.
