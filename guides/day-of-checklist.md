# Day-of Cleanup Checklist

## Before Leaving Home (organizer)
- [ ] Check weather — have a rain plan ready
- [ ] Charge phone for photos/communication
- [ ] Pack supplies: trash bags, gloves, grabbers, hand sanitizer, first aid kit, sunscreen
- [ ] Print sign-in sheet (backup for digital signup)
- [ ] Bring water bottles or remind volunteers to bring water
- [ ] Have emergency contact info accessible

## Arrival (30 min early)
- [ ] Scout the area for safety hazards (broken glass, needles, unstable ground)
- [ ] Set up meeting point with visible marker/sign
- [ ] Take “before” photos from multiple angles
- [ ] Test that signup form/check-in works
- [ ] Identify trash staging area (where full bags will be collected)

## Volunteer Arrival
- [ ] Greet each volunteer warmly
- [ ] Check them in (sign-in sheet or digital)
- [ ] Brief safety talk (what NOT to pick up: needles, chemicals, heavy items)
- [ ] Distribute supplies (bags, gloves, grabbers)
- [ ] Assign areas or let volunteers choose
- [ ] Point out restroom locations
- [ ] Set end time expectation

## During Cleanup
- [ ] Float between groups to check in
- [ ] Take photos and action shots
- [ ] Collect full bags at staging area
- [ ] Count bags/boxes as they accumulate
- [ ] Watch for volunteer fatigue — remind people to take breaks and hydrate
- [ ] Be welcoming if passersby show interest

## Wrap-Up
- [ ] Gather group for final count of bags collected
- [ ] Take “after” photos from same vantage points as “before”
- [ ] Group photo (with permission)
- [ ] Thank everyone verbally
- [ ] Collect any borrowed equipment
- [ ] Ensure all trash bags are in the designated pickup area
- [ ] Note anything that needs city attention (broken fixtures, illegal dumping)

## After Event
- [ ] Send thank-you within 48 hours
- [ ] Upload photos to shared album
- [ ] Complete post-event report
- [ ] Share results on social media
- [ ] Follow up with any city/parks department contacts

## Pro Tips from Devoe Park
“Our cleanup took about 1 hour with 5 volunteers. We used a wheelchair as a mobile supply station — get creative with what you have! We found that sidewalks and playground areas had the most litter. A few local residents said hello, which was encouraging for community building.”
