# Evidence Collection & Photo Documentation Guide

## Why Document?
Clear documentation provides accountability, enables before/after comparisons, creates social media content, supports grant applications, and helps engage city or parks departments.

## Before Photos (Essential)
- Take wide-angle shots of the full area.
- Capture close-ups of the worst spots (litter accumulation, damage).
- Include recognizable landmarks for before/after comparison.
- Photograph entrance, playgrounds, paths, benches, and trash cans.
- Take photos from consistent vantage points you can replicate after.
- Note the date, time, and weather conditions.

## During the Cleanup
- Action shots of volunteers working.
- Interesting or unusual finds.
- Full trash bags (shows volume collected).
- Progress shots from the same vantage points as “before.”

## After Photos
- Same vantage points as before photos.
- Clean areas that were previously littered.
- Group photo of volunteers (with permission).
- Bags/boxes of collected trash lined up.

## Technical Tips
- Use a phone camera with location/timestamp enabled.
- Shoot in landscape orientation for wider coverage.
- Avoid shooting into the sun.
- Upload to a shared album or folder the same day while memories are fresh.

## Organizing Your Evidence
- Create a folder structure: `evidence/[park-name]/[date]/before/`, `during/`, `after/`
- Include a text report alongside photos.
- Use our post-event report template.

## Privacy Considerations
Get permission before photographing people’s faces, especially minors. Public parks generally allow photography, but always be respectful.

## Real Example (Devoe Park)
During the Devoe Park cleanup (Feb 2026), before/after photos of playgrounds and sidewalks helped demonstrate the impact of removing ~180 gallons of trash and were reused for outreach and volunteer recruitment.
